# How a library lends its books

This document is long on purpose. It is used to check what happens when a whole document is larger than the context of a model on your computer. The library it describes is made up.

## 1. Membership cards

This section explains how a membership card works at the Lindenmoor Public Library, a library that exists only in this example. Consider a reader who moves to another district: the staff write the case down, decide what to do, and tell the reader once the decision is final. The library prefers a clear answer given a day later to a quick answer that has to be taken back. When the rules for a membership card change, the notice board at the entrance shows the new rule for a month before it applies.

Readers who disagree with a decision can ask the head librarian, who answers in writing within two weeks. The front desk keeps a small folder of past cases, so that similar cases are treated in a similar way. If a reader who moves to another district happens again in the same year, the staff look at the earlier note before they decide. The field `card_number` in the circulation system holds the value that the staff check first.

Branch managers meet every spring to compare how each branch handles a membership card, and they agree on any change together. In short, a membership card is meant to be simple for readers and predictable for the staff who apply it.

- Ask at the front desk about a membership card.
- Write down the date when a reader who moves to another district comes up.
- Check `card_number` before you change anything by hand.
